A small browser tool for marking up architectural renders and PDFs: clean pill callouts with leader lines, freehand and straight-line sketching, dimensions that give real lengths, and export to PNG, JPG or PDF, at full quality or as a smaller file.

**Use it:** https://samiabdulnour.github.io/render-markup/

Everything runs in your browser. Images and PDFs never leave your computer. The optional grammar check sends only the note text to [LanguageTool](https://languagetool.org).

Your last settings are kept in your browser for next time: the pen and the highlighter (colour, width, tip, and which one was in use), the eraser size, the settings for a new note (colour, shape, orientation and text size), the colour, text size and units of dimensions, the scale, Show line length, the typeface, the shape last drawn, and Legacy controls. It always opens on Draw. Each person's browser keeps its own. Images, PDFs and notes are never stored. In a private window, or with site data blocked, the page starts with the usual settings every time.

## How it works

- **New** (first in the header) starts afresh on a blank canvas, asking first if there is markup to lose.
- **Open** (in the header; Open file on the blank canvas) starts a new markup from a render or a PDF (the button, or drag and drop). Pick or drop several files at once (PDFs and images, in any mix) and they open as one document, in that order: a way to merge PDFs, or to make one PDF of several images. It starts on a blank white canvas showing a drop placeholder; drop a render onto it, or just start marking up the blank canvas and the placeholder gets out of the way. If there is markup now, on any page, it asks before clearing it, and No leaves everything as it was.
- **Add image**, at the top of the Move tool's panel, puts a picture, such as a screenshot, onto the page you are on, and the markup stays. Given a PDF, it adds that PDF's pages after the page you are on instead. Ctrl/Cmd+V does the same with a picture on the clipboard. (Only on the empty blank canvas, before anything is drawn, does a pasted picture open as the image to mark up.) It goes in the middle of the view, at its own size or at most half the page across or down, and Move comes on so it can be dragged into place straight away. A picture lies on the page under the drawing and the notes, so lines and notes can go over it; the eraser never rubs it out.
- **Move:** the first button in the tool bar (V). With it on, the left button (or a finger) drags pictures, and notes by their pill or dot; a click selects a picture, a click on nothing lets it go. A selected picture has handles: drag a corner or an edge (anywhere along it) to resize it freely, or hold Shift to keep its proportions; drag the round handle above it to turn it about its middle (it settles square when close, and Shift turns it in steps of 15°). The Move panel lists the page's pictures with a thumbnail of each, the one in front at the top as in a layers list: click one to select it, drag one up or down the list to change which lies in front, or use Bring forward and Send backward (Ctrl/Cmd+] and Ctrl/Cmd+[). Rotate left and Rotate right give it a quarter turn, and Delete picture (or the Delete key) takes it away. With the other tools a drag over a picture draws, measures or erases as anywhere else. **Outline** gives the selected picture a thin or thick frame in one of the colours, or None. Undo covers all of it, each PDF page keeps its own pictures, and they are in the export.
- **PDFs:** every page opens, with a strip of page thumbnails down the left (across the top on a phone). Click a thumbnail, press PageDown / PageUp, or turn the mouse wheel to move between pages: the wheel reads through the document as a PDF viewer does, down a page and on to the next, a page a turn when the page fits the screen. Zoomed in, a page of another size keeps the paper the same size on screen. Each page has its own notes, drawing and undo history, and its thumbnail shows a marker with the number of notes on it, or a dot if it only has drawing. Pages are drawn at about 4000 pixels on the long side, as they are needed. To change the order, drag a thumbnail up or down the strip (a line shows where it will go). Deleting a page with markup asks first, the last page cannot be deleted, and deleting cannot be undone. The export follows the strip. PDFs and images dropped on the strip go in as pages where they are dropped (a line shows where). Drag the strip's right edge (or use the arrow keys on it) to make it wider or narrower, from 90 to 480 px; wider thumbnails are drawn sharper, and the width is kept for next time.
- **Options:** the gear at the foot of the tool bar, under the Eraser (or ? on the keyboard). It holds **Legacy controls**, on to begin with and kept for next time, which decides what the mouse buttons do, as follows; under it, **Shortcuts**, folded away until clicked (? opens Options with them unfolded): every shortcut and what each mouse button does, written for the switch as it is.
- **Mouse,** with Legacy controls on: the left button draws; the wheel button (middle click) erases; dragging with the right button adds a note, and a plain right-click opens a pop-up menu. That is the same whichever tool is on, except that the left button erases with the Eraser on and draws dimensions with Measure on. Pills and their dots can be dragged with the left or right button.
- **Right-click menu:** on a note it offers Edit text, the colours, the four shapes, Make vertical / horizontal, Make outline / filled, Add arrow (on a note whose dot is under it) and Delete; if the note is part of a selection, these apply to all of it. On a picture it offers Bring forward, Send backward and Delete picture first. Elsewhere it offers Add note here, Delete this line (when a drawn line is under the pointer; on a dimension, Set its length and Delete this dimension), Select all notes, Undo, Redo, Clear drawing, Save image and Copy to clipboard. On a laptop trackpad a two-finger tap opens it, so Add note here works without dragging.
- **Tool wheel:** another way of working, for those who pick a tool first: switch **Legacy controls** off in Options, and a click with the wheel button opens the tools in a ring round the pointer, as in Grasshopper: Move, Draw, Highlighter, Shapes, Notes, Measure and Eraser, the one that is on ringed and the name of the one pointed at underneath. Only the round tool buttons show, with no disc behind them. Click a tool to turn it on, or hold the wheel button, move towards a tool and let go. A click in its middle or far outside it, a right click, the mouse wheel or Esc closes it with nothing changed, and a tool key (D, N...) closes it and does its job. Near an edge it moves in so all of it shows. Working this way each tool works with the left button: with Notes on, a left-drag from a spot places a note there with its pill where you let go, and a left click puts one there with its arrow, text ready; the right button only opens the menu (a right-drag does nothing). The switch is kept for next time.
- **Eraser:** the left button with the Eraser on (handy on a laptop or a touch screen), or the wheel button while **Legacy controls** is on in Options (as it is to begin with; switched off, the wheel button opens the tool wheel instead); with the Eraser on, its outline follows the pointer. It rubs out from the moment it is pressed, and exactly the area it passes over, as an eraser on paper does: a line can be trimmed or cut in two, a sliver taken off the edge of a thick line, or a channel run down its middle. What is left of a line looks just as it did: its dashes, a cloud's scallops and a shape's corners stay exactly where they were. Ink drawn afterwards is not touched by an earlier pass, and a line rubbed out completely is gone from the drawing. A dimension goes whole. It never rubs out a picture added to the page. It also removes any note whose pill it touches; the leader and dot do not count, so a sketch around a dot can be rubbed out without losing the note. Undo brings everything back. With Legacy controls on, hold Shift while dragging with the wheel button for a straight eraser, which snaps to horizontal, vertical and 45° like the straight pen. The Eraser size slider in the Eraser panel sets how wide it is; it starts at 100 px. Its shape is set in the Eraser panel too: Round, or Square (an upright square of the same width, which also rubs out what is in its corners). Its outline on the image shows which one it is.
- **Changing sizes with the mouse:** hold Ctrl (Alt also works) and drag with a mouse button, as in Photoshop, to resize what that button does instead of using it. Dragging sideways and dragging up and down both work: right or up is bigger, left or down is smaller, and the two add up. The line width doubles for every 120 px moved right or up and halves for every 120 px left or down; text and eraser sizes change evenly. The left button changes the line width (the eraser size with the Eraser on, the dimension text with Measure on), the wheel button the eraser size (with Legacy controls on) and the right button the note text size. The cursor stays where it is while you do this: only the size changes, and the cursor is back in the same spot when you let go. With the right button it works like adding a note: on empty image the new note shows where it will go while you set its size, and when you let go it is added at that size with its text ready to type. Started on an existing note, it resizes that note, or the whole selection it belongs to. After one note is resized, this way or with the Text size slider, the keyboard is in its text box, so you can type straight away. A preview shows the size: the pen tip at its real size, the eraser ring, or the note itself, with a label giving the eraser and text sizes in pixels. The sliders follow. The Line width and Eraser size sliders show the same preview in the middle of the image while they are moved. Line width and text size are shown in pixels of the image, so the same slider position reads differently on a larger image; the eraser size is in pixels on screen.
- **Zoom:** hold Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) and turn the mouse wheel to zoom in and out at the pointer; a trackpad pinch does the same, and so do the + and - keys (about the middle). It goes in to 800% and out past the fitted size to a tenth of it, to make the image small on the stage. When zoomed in, the wheel scrolls up and down, Shift + wheel scrolls sideways, and holding Space lets you drag the image with the left button. The read-out in the top-right corner of the image shows the scale; click it, or press 0, to fit the image again. Zoom only changes the view: the export is always the full image.
- **Tools:** Move, Draw, Highlighter, Shapes, Notes, Measure and Eraser sit in a small bar floating at the top-left of the image (V, D, H, S, N, M and E from the keyboard). The image is fitted to the right of the bar, so the bar only covers it when zoomed in, and then the image can be moved out from under it. Each tool shows its own settings in the side panel, and the panel follows what you do: it goes to Notes when you add or pick up a note, and to Draw when you draw. On a touch screen, where there are no other buttons, the tool also says what a finger does: it draws, erases, places a note, or measures. The page always opens on Draw.
- **Measure:** with Measure on, drag with the left button to draw a dimension: a line with a bar and a 45° tick at each end and its length written along it. Close to level, upright or 45° it snaps there; hold Shift to make it snap always. Type the drawing's scale in the panel, such as 1:100 (or just 100), and every dimension shows its real length; until then they read in pixels. On a PDF the page size makes this exact, as long as the drawing was printed to scale. An image has no paper size, so its resolution is used: it is read from the file when the file says (PNG and JPEG), otherwise it is taken as 96 dpi, and it can be corrected in the panel. For an image with no real scale, such as a render, right-click a dimension and choose Set its length to type how long it really is; that sets the scale instead. The scale is one setting: it stays the same from page to page, carries on to the next drawing you open, and is kept for next time. A page that already has dimensions keeps the scale they were placed at, so changing the scale on another page leaves those dimensions as they are; changing it on that page changes them. Right-click a dimension to set its length or to delete it. Units are millimetres, centimetres or metres. The eraser rubs out a dimension whole, Clear drawing removes dimensions with the rest of the drawing, and Ctrl + left-drag sets the size of the dimension text.
- **Notes:** press the right button on the spot you want to describe and drag out to where the note should sit. Add note here in the right-click menu places one automatically. Type the text in the side panel; the pill resizes itself. Hold Shift while you drag a note out, or while you move its pill or its dot, to keep its line straight: horizontal, vertical or at 45°, whichever is nearest. To write in a note later, click it on the image, or select it and press Enter (or F2): the cursor goes to the end of its text, ready to type. After changing a note's typeface, colour, shape or orientation the cursor goes back to its text too, so typing carries on in the note. Esc finishes and lets the note go.
- **A comment without an arrow:** every note starts with its arrow. To leave one without, drag its dot under its own pill: the line and the dot go, the note stays as a plain comment, and it moves as one piece. Add arrow in the right-click menu brings the dot back out.
- **Selecting several notes:** in the note list (at the foot of the Notes panel, under the settings; Check grammar stays at its top), Shift-click selects everything between the note you clicked last and this one; Ctrl/Cmd-click adds or removes a single note; Ctrl/Cmd+A selects every note. Colour, shape, orientation, text size and Delete then apply to all of them, and dragging one selected pill moves them all.
- **Dot or arrow end:** a note's line ends in a dot on the spot, or with **Arrow end** (Notes panel, or the right-click menu) in an arrowhead pointing at it.
- **Filled or outline:** a note is filled with its colour, its words in white (dark on yellow and white), or, with **Outline** (in the Notes panel, or Make outline in the right-click menu), an outline in its colour round its words in its colour, white inside; a yellow one is white inside too, with dark words (yellow words on white would not read), and a white one is dark inside. Like the other settings it applies to the selected notes, or with none selected to the next one, and is kept for next time.
- **Note shapes:** pill, rectangle, rectangle with rounded corners, or circle. A circle suits short labels such as a number or a letter. With no note selected, the Notes tab shows the settings for the next note (colour, shape, orientation and text size), so they can be picked before it is placed. They start as those of the note selected last.
- **Note colours:** red (the default), black, white, green, yellow, blue or magenta, or **your own**: the last swatch, a ring of colours, opens the colour picker (any colour, by eye or by its RGB or hex values), and the colour picked shows in its middle. A yellow note has dark text, like a white one, and so does a light colour of your own. Horizontal by default, vertical optional. One Text size slider sets the size of the selected note or notes; to resize every note, select them all with Ctrl/Cmd+A first. Long notes wrap on their own; press Enter in the text box for a line break exactly where you want one.
- **Draw:** freehand pen in black, white, red, green, yellow, blue or magenta, or a colour of your own (the last swatch; every colour row has it: pen, highlighter, notes, dimensions and picture outlines). The colour picked last is kept for next time, and a whole go at the picker is one step to undo. Hold Shift for a straight line, which snaps to horizontal, vertical and 45°. The Line width slider runs from very thin to about 5% of the image width (roughly 1 to 100 px on a 1920 px render) on a scale that keeps fine control over thin lines.
- **Show line length:** Show line length in the Draw panel is on to begin with: while you draw a line with Shift its length shows along it, in the pen colour. Switching it on with no scale set asks for one right there: a Scale field under the switch, such as 1:100. It is the same scale as under Measure, so setting it in either place sets both. Until a scale is set, lengths show in pixels. It is only a read-out while drawing: it goes when you let go and is never part of the image. The switch is kept for next time.
- **Shapes:** a tool of its own in the tool bar, under the Highlighter (S from the keyboard). Its panel starts with what to draw: a **rectangle**, a **rounded rectangle**, a **pill** or a **circle** (each dragged from corner to corner, Shift for a square or a true circle). They take the line's colour, width, tip and style, so a shape can be dashed, dotted or a cloud, and work with the highlighter too. The eraser cuts them like any line, and right-click offers Delete this line. The choice is kept for next time.
- **Arrow end:** beside the tip in the Draw panel, a plain end or an **arrow end**: any pen or highlighter line, freehand or straight, solid, dashed or a cloud, then ends in a solid arrowhead along the way it last went, growing with the line's width. The pen and the highlighter each keep their own, and it is kept for next time.
- **Line style:** under the tip, five pictures: solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot, and **Cloud**. Dashes and dots are in steps of the line's width, round or square with the tip. Cloud draws a revision cloud, as CAD marks a change: the line becomes a row of round scallops bulging outwards, of uneven size as if drawn by hand (the same line always gets the same cloud), and a line drawn round an area that ends near where it began closes into a loop (with Shift, a straight row of scallops). The pen and the highlighter each keep their own style, it is kept for next time, and what the eraser leaves of a line keeps its style exactly.
- **Round or square tip:** the square tip is a square brush, as in a paint program: an upright square swept along the line. Lines end square, a turn has a sharp outer corner, a single click leaves a square, and a slanted line comes out a little wider than a level one. With the round tip, ends and turns are rounded. It applies to the pen and the highlighter separately.
- **Highlighter:** a button of its own in the tool bar, under Draw (H from the keyboard), draws a wide see-through line instead of the pen's, for marking an area without hiding it; what is underneath shows through. It starts yellow and wide, and the pen and the highlighter each remember their own colour, width and tip. Everything else works the same on it: straight lines with Shift, the eraser, undo, and the export.
- **Typeface:** under All notes, for notes and lengths: Arial (the one to begin with), Arial Narrow, Verdana, Open Sans, Menlo and ISOCPEUR, each written in its own face in the list. ISOCPEUR, the ISO 3098 lettering of CAD drawings, is AutoCAD's own where the computer has it (it cannot be served from a website); elsewhere the page uses osifont, a free ISO 3098 lettering served with it (`fonts/`, LGPL with the font exception). Each is the computer's own where it has it (Menlo on a Mac; Consolas, its like, on Windows); Open Sans comes from Google Fonts, and a computer without Arial Narrow or a mono gets a web copy of one like it. Under it, **B** sets every note and length in the bold of the typeface, and **Aa / AA / aa** sets every note as typed, in capitals or in lower case; the words as typed are kept, so Aa brings them back. All of it is kept for next time. Lengths on dimensions are plain text in their colour, with no outline.
- **Check grammar** (at the top of the Notes panel): proofreads every note (Australian English). Review each suggestion before it applies; names and street names start unticked.
- **Edit** (in the header) opens the editing menu: Edit text… (on a PDF page), Fill in form… (for a PDF with a form), Rotate right and left, Crop page…, Redact…, Add pages…, Duplicate page, Delete page and Compare with….
- **Page tools:** the same for any page from its thumbnail: right-click it for Rotate right, Rotate left, Crop page…, Redact…, Add pages after this…, Duplicate page, Move up, Move down and Delete page. They work on an image too, which then becomes the first page of a document.
  - **Rotate** turns the page a quarter turn with its markup, which keeps its size.
  - **Crop page…** dims the page: drag a frame over the part to keep, then drag the frame, or its edges and corners, to adjust; Apply (or Enter) crops, Cancel (or Esc) leaves it. Markup outside the frame is kept out of sight; **Remove crop** in the menu brings the whole page back, the markup where it was. A cropped PDF page stays sharp: the export sets its crop box.
  - **Add pages** takes PDFs and images (several at once) and puts their pages in after the page.
  - **Duplicate page** puts a copy in after it, turned and cropped as it is, with a copy of its markup to change separately.
  - **Redact…** blacks out what must not be seen: drag a box over each thing (a click on a box takes it off, Backspace the last one), then Apply (or Enter). The boxes are black on the page at once; in the exported PDF that page is saved as a picture, so the text and drawing under them are gone from the file, not just covered. **Remove redactions** takes them off before then.
- **Compare with…** (Edit menu) compares the document with another version of it, a PDF or an image, page by page: the areas that differ are ringed in magenta, and the bar at the top says how many there are on the page. **This / Other / Overlay** chooses what shows: this file's page, the other version's in its place (to flick between them), or both laid over each other as in ArchiCAD's Trace and Reference, this file in blue and the other in red, each with a slider for how strong it is (a click on a colour dot changes it): where the two agree the ink mixes dark, and what only one has shows in its colour; **Mark with clouds** draws a red revision cloud round every change, on every page, in a fine line whatever the pen is set to, and at least three scallops across (one undo a page); Close ends it.
- **Edit text…** (Edit menu, on a PDF page) changes what the PDF already says. Each line of its words gets a faint outline; click one and type over it (Enter keeps it, Esc leaves it), or empty it to take the words off. Click a clear spot to add words there, or drag over an area to white it out. The old words are covered in the colour round them and the new ones set at their size, place and colour, in the PDF's own font when it has every letter typed (a PDF usually holds only the letters it uses, so fixing a typo, a number or a date normally keeps the font), otherwise in the nearest standard face (sans, serif or mono, bold and italic as they were), and the message says which. Words added on a clear spot take the font, size and colour of the words nearest to them. Each change can be undone; Esc or Done ends it. In the exported PDF the new words are real text, in the PDF's own font or a standard PDF face (words with letters neither has, such as ř in a standard face, go in as a sharp picture), and the old ones are covered over, not deleted: they can still be found by copying the PDF's text. To take words out for good, use Redact.
- **Fill in form…** appears in the Edit menu for a PDF with a form (it says so when it opens): its fields, page by page and named plainly, as text boxes, switches and lists. What you fill in shows on the page as you go and goes into the exported PDF, which stays a form.
- **Export:** the button (and Ctrl/Cmd+S, and Export… in the right-click menu) asks how to save:
  - **Pages** (for a document of more than one page): All pages, This page, or Choose, with a list such as `1-3, 5` (in that order; a page that is not there is refused). **One file a page** saves each page as a file of its own, all in one `.zip`: a way to split a PDF, or to save every page as a picture.
  - **PNG**, at full resolution, every pixel kept: `<name>_markup.png`, or `<name>_p<page>_markup.png` for one page of a document; several pages come as a `.zip` of PNGs.
  - **JPG**, at full resolution on white, a smaller file than PNG: `<name>_markup.jpg`; several pages as a `.zip` of JPGs.
  - **PDF, full quality:** for a PDF, `<name>_markup.pdf` is the original PDF, pages untouched and still sharp, with your markup added on top of the pages that have any, in the order of the strip, each turned and cropped as shown. An image page is the image at full resolution on a page the size it prints at (from its resolution), and the blank canvas a white page. One PDF exported whole, with nothing moved, added or deleted, is kept as it is, its links and form fields with it. For an image, a one-page PDF the size the image prints at, the image at full resolution.
  - **PDF, compact:** as full quality, the lines and text still sharp vectors, with the pictures in it made lighter: photos and scans larger than 2000 px across become JPEGs of that size, and anything stored unpacked is packed. Much smaller for a PDF of photos or scans; about the same for one of plain lines and text. For an image, a one-page PDF with the image as a JPEG at full resolution.
  - **PDF, smallest:** each page as a picture at about 150 dpi with the markup on it: usually far smaller, but text and lines are no longer sharp when zoomed in, nor can they be selected. If that would not be smaller (a PDF of plain vector drawings often is not), the full-quality PDF is saved instead and a message says so.

  A PDF that cannot be edited, such as an encrypted one, is saved as pictures of its pages instead, and a message says so. Save image (Save this page as an image on a PDF) in the right-click menu saves a PNG straight away.
- **Copy to clipboard:** in the right-click menu, or Ctrl/Cmd+C, puts the full-resolution marked-up image on the clipboard, ready to paste into a chat, an email or a document.

## Look

The logo is in `brand/`: the original (`logo.png`), and traced from it as vectors in black (`logo.svg`) and white (`logo-white.svg`). In the app it is drawn in the text colour, so it follows the theme.


The interface follows Loriini's: a light grey surround with the settings on white cards, a one-pixel edge, flat, with 8 px corners on controls and 12 px on cards. What is chosen or switched on is pale lemon with ink words, and Export is the one lemon button. The type is set like Site Analyst's: Arial at one weight, never bold, and at one size, 14px, for every word of the interface, with the labels, buttons and panel headings in capitals spaced a little apart. Only the drop placeholder has its own: a 30px headline, an 18px Open file button and 12px notes. Every word is black, hints and labels too (a button that cannot be used is the same black, faded). The note shapes, and the choices in the panels (tip, eraser shape, orientation (a note reading across, or upwards), filled or outline, dot or arrow end, layer order, turning a picture, its outline), are pictograms only, named in their tooltips, like the colours. The typefaces for notes are chosen separately under All notes. Colours are picked from plain solid circles (their names show as tooltips), the chosen one ringed. It has no dark version: it looks the same on a system set to dark. Sliders have their name and value on one line, the value at the right, over a thin track inked up to a round knob in the switch's colours; a click leaves no box round them.

There is no footer and no hint line over the image. **Options** (the gear under the Eraser, or ? on the keyboard) lists every shortcut and what each mouse button does, written for the settings as they are (with the tool wheel or without). What the page has to say (saved, copied, a picture added, a file it could not read) shows for a moment as a message at the foot of the stage, then goes. A yellow, white or other light dimension has no dark outline round its line, as its length has none.

## Hosting

Static files with no build step, served by GitHub Pages from the `main` branch root: `index.html` is the whole tool, and `vendor/` holds the two libraries it uses for PDFs ([PDF.js](https://mozilla.github.io/pdf.js/) to read them and [pdf-lib](https://pdf-lib.js.org/) to write them). They are part of this site, so nothing is loaded from elsewhere, and the browser only fetches them when a PDF is opened or exported. See `vendor/README.md` for versions and licences.
