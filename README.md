A small browser tool for marking up architectural renders and PDFs: clean pill callouts with leader lines, freehand and straight-line sketching, dimensions that give real lengths, a full-resolution PNG export for images and a marked-up PDF export for PDFs.

**Use it:** https://samiabdulnour.github.io/render-markup/

Everything runs in your browser. Images and PDFs never leave your computer. The optional grammar check sends only the note text to [LanguageTool](https://languagetool.org).

Your last settings are kept in your browser for next time: the pen and the highlighter (colour, width, tip, and which one was in use), the eraser size, the settings for a new note (colour, shape, orientation and text size), the colour, text size and units of dimensions, the scale, Show line length, and the typeface. It always opens on Draw. Each person's browser keeps its own. Images, PDFs and notes are never stored. In a private window, or with site data blocked, the page starts with the usual settings every time.

## How it works

- **Open file** starts a new markup from a render or a PDF (the button, or drag and drop). It starts on a blank white canvas showing a drop placeholder; drop a render onto it, or just start marking up the blank canvas and the placeholder gets out of the way. If there is markup now, on any page, it asks before clearing it, and No leaves everything as it was.
- **Add image** puts a picture, such as a screenshot, onto the page you are on, and the markup stays. Ctrl/Cmd+V does the same with a picture on the clipboard. (Only on the empty blank canvas, before anything is drawn, does a pasted picture open as the image to mark up.) It goes in the middle of the view, at its own size or at most half the page across or down, and Move comes on so it can be dragged into place straight away. A picture lies on the page under the drawing and the notes, so lines and notes can go over it; the eraser never rubs it out.
- **Move:** the first button in the tool bar (V). With it on, the left button (or a finger) drags pictures, and notes by their pill or dot; a click selects a picture, a click on nothing lets it go. A selected picture has handles: drag a corner or an edge (anywhere along it) to resize it freely, or hold Shift to keep its proportions; drag the round handle above it to turn it about its middle (it settles square when close, and Shift turns it in steps of 15°). The Move panel lists the page's pictures with a thumbnail of each, the one in front at the top as in a layers list: click one to select it, drag one up or down the list to change which lies in front, or use Bring forward and Send backward (Ctrl/Cmd+] and Ctrl/Cmd+[). Rotate left and Rotate right give it a quarter turn, and Delete picture (or the Delete key) takes it away. With the other tools a drag over a picture draws, measures or erases as anywhere else. Undo covers all of it, each PDF page keeps its own pictures, and they are in the export.
- **PDFs:** every page opens, with a strip of page thumbnails down the left (across the top on a phone). Click a thumbnail, press PageDown / PageUp, or turn the mouse wheel to move between pages: the wheel reads through the document as a PDF viewer does, down a page and on to the next, a page a turn when the page fits the screen. Zoomed in, a page of another size keeps the paper the same size on screen. Each page has its own notes, drawing and undo history, and its thumbnail shows a marker with the number of notes on it, or a dot if it only has drawing. Pages are drawn at about 4000 pixels on the long side, as they are needed.
- **Mouse:** the left button draws; the wheel button (middle click) erases; dragging with the right button adds a note, and a plain right-click opens a pop-up menu. That is the same whichever tool is on, except that the left button erases with the Eraser on and draws dimensions with Measure on. Pills and their dots can be dragged with the left or right button.
- **Right-click menu:** on a note it offers Edit text, the five colours, the four shapes, Make vertical / horizontal, Add arrow (on a note whose dot is under it) and Delete; if the note is part of a selection, these apply to all of it. On a picture it offers Bring forward, Send backward and Delete picture first. Elsewhere it offers Add note here, Delete this line (when a drawn line is under the pointer; on a dimension, Set its length and Delete this dimension), Select all notes, Undo, Redo, Clear drawing, Save image and Copy to clipboard. On a laptop trackpad a two-finger tap opens it, so Add note here works without dragging.
- **Tool wheel:** another way of working, for those who pick a tool first: switch **Wheel button erases** off in the Eraser panel, and a click with the wheel button opens the tools in a ring round the pointer, as in Grasshopper: Move, Draw, Highlighter, Notes, Measure and Eraser, the one that is on ringed and the name of the one pointed at underneath. Click a tool to turn it on, or hold the wheel button, move towards a tool and let go. A click in its middle or far outside it, a right click, the mouse wheel or Esc closes it with nothing changed, and a tool key (D, N...) closes it and does its job. Near an edge it moves in so all of it shows. Working this way each tool works with the left button: with Notes on, a left-drag from a spot places a note there with its pill where you let go, and a left click puts one there with its arrow, text ready; the right button only opens the menu (a right-drag does nothing). The switch is kept for next time.
- **Eraser:** the left button with the Eraser on (handy on a laptop or a touch screen), or the wheel button while **Wheel button erases** is on in the Eraser panel (as it is to begin with; switched off, the wheel button opens the tool wheel instead); with the Eraser on, its outline follows the pointer. It rubs out from the moment it is pressed, and exactly the area it passes over, as an eraser on paper does: a line can be trimmed or cut in two, a sliver taken off the edge of a thick line, or a channel run down its middle. Ink drawn afterwards is not touched by an earlier pass, and a line rubbed out completely is gone from the drawing. A dimension goes whole. It never rubs out a picture added to the page. It also removes any note whose pill it touches; the leader and dot do not count, so a sketch around a dot can be rubbed out without losing the note. Undo brings everything back. With Wheel button erases on, hold Shift while dragging with the wheel button for a straight eraser, which snaps to horizontal, vertical and 45° like the straight pen. The Eraser size slider in the Eraser panel sets how wide it is; it starts at 100 px. Its shape is set in the Eraser panel too: Round, or Square (an upright square of the same width, which also rubs out what is in its corners). Its outline on the image shows which one it is.
- **Changing sizes with the mouse:** hold Ctrl (Alt also works) and drag with a mouse button, as in Photoshop, to resize what that button does instead of using it. Dragging sideways and dragging up and down both work: right or up is bigger, left or down is smaller, and the two add up. The line width doubles for every 120 px moved right or up and halves for every 120 px left or down; text and eraser sizes change evenly. The left button changes the line width (the eraser size with the Eraser on, the dimension text with Measure on), the wheel button the eraser size (with Wheel button erases on) and the right button the note text size. The cursor stays where it is while you do this: only the size changes, and the cursor is back in the same spot when you let go. With the right button it works like adding a note: on empty image the new note shows where it will go while you set its size, and when you let go it is added at that size with its text ready to type. Started on an existing note, it resizes that note, or the whole selection it belongs to. After one note is resized, this way or with the Text size slider, the keyboard is in its text box, so you can type straight away. A preview shows the size: the pen tip at its real size, the eraser ring, or the note itself, with a label giving the eraser and text sizes in pixels. The sliders follow. The Line width and Eraser size sliders show the same preview in the middle of the image while they are moved. Line width and text size are shown in pixels of the image, so the same slider position reads differently on a larger image; the eraser size is in pixels on screen.
- **Zoom:** hold Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) and turn the mouse wheel to zoom in and out at the pointer; a trackpad pinch does the same, and so do the + and - keys (about the middle). It goes in to 800% and out past the fitted size to a tenth of it, to make the image small on the stage. When zoomed in, the wheel scrolls up and down, Shift + wheel scrolls sideways, and holding Space lets you drag the image with the left button. The read-out in the top-right corner of the image shows the scale; click it, or press 0, to fit the image again. Zoom only changes the view: the export is always the full image.
- **Tools:** Move, Draw, Highlighter, Notes, Measure and Eraser sit in a small bar floating at the top-left of the image (V, D, H, N, M and E from the keyboard). The image is fitted to the right of the bar, so the bar only covers it when zoomed in, and then the image can be moved out from under it. Each tool shows its own settings in the side panel, and the panel follows what you do: it goes to Notes when you add or pick up a note, and to Draw when you draw. On a touch screen, where there are no other buttons, the tool also says what a finger does: it draws, erases, places a note, or measures. The page always opens on Draw.
- **Measure:** with Measure on, drag with the left button to draw a dimension: a line with a bar and a 45° tick at each end and its length written along it. Close to level, upright or 45° it snaps there; hold Shift to make it snap always. Type the drawing's scale in the panel, such as 1:100 (or just 100), and every dimension shows its real length; until then they read in pixels. On a PDF the page size makes this exact, as long as the drawing was printed to scale. An image has no paper size, so its resolution is used: it is read from the file when the file says (PNG and JPEG), otherwise it is taken as 96 dpi, and it can be corrected in the panel. For an image with no real scale, such as a render, right-click a dimension and choose Set its length to type how long it really is; that sets the scale instead. The scale is one setting: it stays the same from page to page, carries on to the next drawing you open, and is kept for next time. A page that already has dimensions keeps the scale they were placed at, so changing the scale on another page leaves those dimensions as they are; changing it on that page changes them. Right-click a dimension to set its length or to delete it. Units are millimetres, centimetres or metres. The eraser rubs out a dimension whole, Clear drawing removes dimensions with the rest of the drawing, and Ctrl + left-drag sets the size of the dimension text.
- **Notes:** press the right button on the spot you want to describe and drag out to where the note should sit. Add note here in the right-click menu places one automatically. Type the text in the side panel; the pill resizes itself. Hold Shift while you drag a note out, or while you move its pill or its dot, to keep its line straight: horizontal, vertical or at 45°, whichever is nearest. To write in a note later, click it on the image, or select it and press Enter (or F2): the cursor goes to the end of its text, ready to type. After changing a note's typeface, colour, shape or orientation the cursor goes back to its text too, so typing carries on in the note. Esc finishes and lets the note go.
- **A comment without an arrow:** every note starts with its arrow. To leave one without, drag its dot under its own pill: the line and the dot go, the note stays as a plain comment, and it moves as one piece. Add arrow in the right-click menu brings the dot back out.
- **Selecting several notes:** in the notes list, Shift-click selects everything between the note you clicked last and this one; Ctrl/Cmd-click adds or removes a single note; Ctrl/Cmd+A selects every note. Colour, shape, orientation, text size and Delete then apply to all of them, and dragging one selected pill moves them all.
- **Note shapes:** pill, rectangle, rectangle with rounded corners, or circle. A circle suits short labels such as a number or a letter. With no note selected, the Notes tab shows the settings for the next note (colour, shape, orientation and text size), so they can be picked before it is placed. They start as those of the note selected last.
- **Note colours:** red (the default), black, white, green, yellow, blue or magenta; a yellow note has dark text, like a white one. Horizontal by default, vertical optional. One Text size slider sets the size of the selected note or notes; to resize every note, select them all with Ctrl/Cmd+A first. Long notes wrap on their own; press Enter in the text box for a line break exactly where you want one.
- **Draw:** freehand pen in black, white, red, green, yellow, blue or magenta. Hold Shift for a straight line, which snaps to horizontal, vertical and 45°. The Line width slider runs from very thin to about 5% of the image width (roughly 1 to 100 px on a 1920 px render) on a scale that keeps fine control over thin lines.
- **Show line length:** Show line length in the Draw panel is on to begin with: while you draw a line with Shift its length shows along it, in the pen colour. Switching it on with no scale set asks for one right there: a Scale field under the switch, such as 1:100. It is the same scale as under Measure, so setting it in either place sets both. Until a scale is set, lengths show in pixels. It is only a read-out while drawing: it goes when you let go and is never part of the image. The switch is kept for next time.
- **Round or square tip:** the square tip is a square brush, as in a paint program: an upright square swept along the line. Lines end square, a turn has a sharp outer corner, a single click leaves a square, and a slanted line comes out a little wider than a level one. With the round tip, ends and turns are rounded. It applies to the pen and the highlighter separately,.
- **Highlighter:** a button of its own in the tool bar, under Draw (H from the keyboard), draws a wide see-through line instead of the pen's, for marking an area without hiding it; what is underneath shows through. It starts yellow and wide, and the pen and the highlighter each remember their own colour, width and tip. Everything else works the same on it: straight lines with Shift, the eraser, undo, and the export.
- **Typeface:** under All notes, for notes and lengths: five usual ones, Arial (the one to begin with), Arial Narrow, Verdana, Open Sans and Menlo, each written in its own face in the list. Each is the computer's own where it has it (Menlo on a Mac; Consolas, its like, on Windows); Open Sans comes from Google Fonts, and a computer without Arial Narrow or a mono gets a web copy of one like it. Under it, **B** sets every note and length in the bold of the typeface, and **Aa / AA / aa** sets every note as typed, in capitals or in lower case; the words as typed are kept, so Aa brings them back. All of it is kept for next time. Lengths on dimensions are plain text in their colour, with no outline.
- **Check grammar:** proofreads every note (Australian English). Review each suggestion before it applies; names and street names start unticked.
- **Export PNG:** saves at the image's full resolution as `<name>_markup.png`. Save image in the right-click menu and Ctrl/Cmd+S do the same.
- **Export PDF:** with a PDF open, the button (and Ctrl/Cmd+S, and Save PDF in the right-click menu) saves `<name>_markup.pdf`: the original PDF, pages untouched and still sharp, with your markup added on top of the pages that have any. A PDF that cannot be edited, such as an encrypted one, is saved as pictures of its pages instead, and a message says so. Save this page as an image in the right-click menu saves just the current page as `<name>_p<page>_markup.png`.
- **Copy to clipboard:** in the right-click menu, or Ctrl/Cmd+C, puts the full-resolution marked-up image on the clipboard, ready to paste into a chat, an email or a document.

## Look

The logo is in `brand/`: the original (`logo.png`), and traced from it as vectors in black (`logo.svg`) and white (`logo-white.svg`). In the app it is drawn in the text colour, so it follows the theme.


The interface follows Loriini's: a light grey surround with the settings on white cards, a one-pixel edge, flat, with 8 px corners on controls and 12 px on cards. What is chosen or switched on is pale lemon with ink words, and Export is the one lemon button. The type is set like Site Analyst's: Arial at one weight, never bold, and at one size, 14px, for every word of the interface, with the labels, buttons and panel headings in capitals spaced a little apart. Only the drop placeholder has its own: a 30px headline, an 18px Open file button and 12px notes. Every word is black, hints and labels too (a button that cannot be used is the same black, faded). The note shapes are pictures only, named in their tooltips, like the colours. The typefaces for notes are chosen separately under All notes. Colours are picked from plain solid circles (their names show as tooltips), the chosen one ringed. It has no dark version: it looks the same on a system set to dark. Sliders have their name and value on one line, the value at the right, over a thin track inked up to a round knob in the switch's colours; a click leaves no box round them.

There is no footer and no hint line over the image. **Shortcuts** in the header (or ? on the keyboard) opens a list of every shortcut and what each mouse button does, written for the settings as they are (with the tool wheel or without). What the page has to say (saved, copied, a picture added, a file it could not read) shows for a moment as a message at the foot of the stage, then goes. A yellow or white dimension has no dark outline round its line, as its length has none.

## Hosting

Static files with no build step, served by GitHub Pages from the `main` branch root: `index.html` is the whole tool, and `vendor/` holds the two libraries it uses for PDFs ([PDF.js](https://mozilla.github.io/pdf.js/) to read them and [pdf-lib](https://pdf-lib.js.org/) to write them). They are part of this site, so nothing is loaded from elsewhere, and the browser only fetches them when a PDF is opened or exported. See `vendor/README.md` for versions and licences.
